# Data — progress-report draft

**Status as of November 3, 2026:** data preparation has been run; source-family review and split freezing remain pending. This draft reports structural audit findings, not model performance. Revise it after family review and actual feedback collection.

We use SpreadsheetBench Verified, an expert-annotated subset of spreadsheet manipulation tasks distributed through the official SpreadsheetBench dataset repository. We downloaded `spreadsheetbench_verified_400.tar.gz` and recorded its SHA-256 digest in the accompanying provenance manifest. The archive contains 400 tasks and 800 workbooks, with one initial workbook and one reference workbook per task. The source metadata identifies 275 cell-level and 125 sheet-level manipulation tasks. We preserve task instructions, identifiers, manipulation types, raw answer-position metadata, and paths to the paired workbooks. Source: [SpreadsheetBench](https://huggingface.co/datasets/KAKA22/SpreadsheetBench/tree/main).

Our automated structural audit checks required metadata, task-ID uniqueness, the presence of an initial/reference pair, and the ability to parse workbook XML. All 400 tasks passed these checks; no tasks were rejected on these grounds. This establishes file availability and structural readability, not the correctness of the reference solutions or compatibility with the final evaluator. Answer-position strings are preserved without silently rewriting their meaning. Workbook formula cells and missing cached values are recorded for subsequent evaluator checks.

We generated a deterministic candidate subset of 120 tasks using the seed `skillshift-candidate-v1`. The candidate contains 78 cell-level and 42 sheet-level tasks. The remaining 280 tasks are recorded as eligible but not sampled, rather than rejected. Selection uses task-family identifiers and deterministic ordering; reference-answer values do not guide selection. The candidate split allocates 60 tasks to development, 30 to validation, and 30 to testing. It is not stratified by task difficulty or manipulation type.

To identify directly shared workbooks, we computed SHA-256 hashes of both the original input bytes and a normalized representation of input cell content. The normalized representation ignores packaging metadata, formatting, and sheet names while retaining cell coordinates, values, and formulas. Shared hashes would connect tasks into source families; the procedure does not merge tasks merely because they request similar operations. No shared raw or normalized input hashes were found, yielding 400 provisionally distinct observed families. This does not establish independent origins: modified workbook variants or common forum-source provenance may be missed. Family assignments and the candidate split therefore remain subject to provenance review before freezing. The merge log is empty because the automated procedure found no merges; no merge examples are claimed.

The split validator checks task uniqueness, the 60/30/30 allocation, and the absence of cross-split observed-family or input-hash overlap. Reference workbooks are stored separately for the evaluator and must not be available to the agent or feedback retrieval. All attempts and variants associated with a reviewed family will remain in the same split. The final accepted manifest and checksums will be recorded before model experiments.

The planned feedback dataset consists of two evaluated attempts for each of the 60 development tasks, giving 120 original attempts. The newer team tracker additionally proposes 24 reruns on four dominant tasks; their role in the intervention still needs specification. Each independent attempt must retain its identity and evaluator-derived outcome. Injected copies will carry separate occurrence IDs linked to the original attempt and will not count as new observations. A tested exact-copy utility preserves original records and matches success/failure proportions exactly, failing when the requested construction is infeasible. Final C0/C1/C2/C3 definitions and real attempts are not yet available. Accordingly, feedback success/failure counts, embeddings, final pool counts, and concentration statistics remain unmeasured and are not reported as results.

The companion `data_summary.csv` supplies the current preparation counts for the report's data table. Add measured feedback-pool statistics after collection; do not substitute unit-test fixtures.
